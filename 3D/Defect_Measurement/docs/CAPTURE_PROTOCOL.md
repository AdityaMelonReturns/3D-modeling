# Capture protocol: rot and holes (depth labels)

**For:** field workers photographing rot or hole defects.
**Why:** we are teaching software to estimate how deep rot and holes go. To do that it needs real
depth readings. You give it those readings with a probe band and one side photo.

**You need:** the blue Teddy Bear ruler, a thin probe or awl, the capture app.

## For every rot or hole defect, do these 4 steps

**1. Straight-on photo (as now).**
- Lay the ruler **flat on the same surface** as the defect, right next to it, not held in the air.
- Stand straight in front of the defect. Fill most of the frame with the defect and ruler.
- Keep fingers and shadows off the defect.

**2. Probe and pick the band.**
Push the probe into the deepest part until it stops. Pick one:

| Band | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **<1 cm** | probe stops almost at the surface |
| **1–3 cm** | stops between 1 and 3 cm in |
| **>3 cm** | goes in more than 3 cm but meets solid wood |
| **through** | goes all the way through |

**3. Side photo with the ruler pushed in.**
- Push the ruler, **bear end outside**, into the deepest part until it stops. Keep it straight in.
- Take the photo **from the side**, about 90° to the ruler, **close enough that the cm marks are
  sharp and readable** (ruler fills at least half the photo).
- The point where the ruler enters the surface must be visible.

**4. Write down the cm mark at the surface.**
Read the ruler number exactly where it enters the surface (for example "12.5") and enter it in the
app or on the sheet. The software checks it against the side photo.

## Also, when possible
- One extra straight-on photo **with flash off** if the first used flash (or the other way round).
- Note the **property ID** for every defect, so photos of one house stay together.

## Do not
- Do not guess the band without probing. If you could not probe, write **"not probed"**.
- Do not hold the ruler in the air for the straight-on photo.
- Do not crop or edit the photos.

---
*Target: 200 labelled defects before the model is trained. ">3 cm" and "through" are rare, so every
one of those is especially valuable.*
